Six things nobody tells you about getting stiff

1

It isn't your age. It's what you stopped doing.

I'm eighty-six and my knees are fine, so age can't be the answer. A joint likes going all the way to the end of where it goes. Most people past sixty haven't fully bent one in about fifteen years.

The body puts away anything you stop asking for. And it puts it away faster than anybody warns you.

2

It goes in an order, and it doesn't start at the knees.

It starts at your feet. Nobody has ever said to me, nurse, my toes have gotten weak. But that's first, and it's why the stairs start feeling different before anything hurts.

Then overhead. Then the floor. Then the low chair — and the low chair is the one people finally notice, by which time you're three doors down from where it started.

3

The floor is always first on the list.

Everybody has a list of things they don't do anymore. Nobody notices it getting longer. Getting down on the ground is usually the first thing on it.

I saw women at seventy-five who couldn't get up off the floor, and every one of them told me it happened suddenly. It never happened suddenly. It took about eleven years and nobody was watching.

4

Every shortcut you're given makes it worse.

The jar opener. The grabber for the top shelf. The hand under your elbow at the curb. Every one of those is somebody being kind to you, which is exactly what makes them so hard to refuse.

Say no anyway. Kindly. They mean well.

5

Ten days in a hospital bed.

That's all it takes to cost an older body a serious amount of leg muscle. She came in for a chest infection. Nothing wrong with her legs. Ten days later she's shuffling and the family says she's had a rough go of it, she's seventy-eight.

She hasn't aged eleven years in two weeks. She's been lying down. I watched that for thirty years, and I was part of it, because we were busy and the bed was easier.

6

You don't need a gym. You need a chair.

A dining chair, a kitchen counter, a doorframe, the bottom stair and two cans of beans. That is the whole equipment list, and every bit of it is already in your house.

I've never had a membership in my life.

Look inside

Six of the thirty days. Every one has the same four parts, and never more than one thing to do.

DAY 01

You Are Not “Just Getting Old”

Today’s Truth

Exhaustion is not a symptom of age. It is almost always a symptom of something specific and fixable, and most doctors never get the time to find out what.

Today’s Practice

Take five minutes today and write down, on actual paper, the three times of day you feel most tired. Just notice. Don’t judge it, and don’t try to fix anything yet.

You are not broken. You are undiagnosed.

DAY 02

The Myth of “Slowing Down”

Today’s Truth

Your body does what you ask of it. Ask less, and it gives you less. Ask the same of it, consistently, and more often than not, it meets you there.

Today’s Practice

Do one physical task the “old you” would have quietly handed off to someone else. Carry the grocery bag yourself. Take the stairs once instead of the elevator.

Slowing down was never a rule your body handed you. It was a rule you handed yourself.

DAY 10

Move Before You Sit

Today’s Truth

Your body wakes up through movement, not through sitting still and waiting for energy to arrive on its own.

Today’s Practice

Before you sit down for breakfast today, do two minutes of gentle movement: stretch your arms overhead, roll your shoulders, or walk to the end of your hallway and back.

You don’t need a gym or a full hour. You need two minutes and the plain willingness to use them.

DAY 11

Protein Before Anything Sweet

Today’s Truth

What you eat in the first hour or two of your day sets the energy pattern for the several hours that follow it.

Today’s Practice

Add one source of real protein to your breakfast today: eggs, plain yogurt, a handful of nuts, or even a slice of last night’s chicken.

You’re not on a diet, and nobody’s asking you to give anything up.

DAY 18

The Racing Mind at Midnight

Today’s Truth

A racing mind at night is usually just unprocessed thoughts looking for somewhere safe to go.

Today’s Practice

Before bed tonight, write down three things on your mind for tomorrow. Get them out of your head and onto actual paper where your mind can trust they’ll still be there.

Your bed is not the place to solve tomorrow’s problems. Paper is.

DAY 25

Build Your Non-Negotiables

Today’s Truth

You don’t need twenty separate habits scattered across your whole day. You need two or three you’ll never skip.

Today’s Practice

Choose your two or three non-negotiables from everything you’ve learned this month. Write them down, in your own words rather than mine.

A few things done consistently will always beat many things done only occasionally.
